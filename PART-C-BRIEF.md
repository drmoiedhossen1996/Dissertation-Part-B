## How to use this document

Each section below gives you five things:

1. **Words** — the budget
2. **Argument** — the single point the section must establish
3. **Facts available** — verified content you can draw on, with the source
4. **Questions** — answer these in order, in your own words, and you have the section
5. **Cut / avoid** — what not to include, and why

Every section except the introduction closes with a **commitment**. This is not optional. Cohort feedback item 3 names its absence as the main failing: *"Reflective cycles not always completed with robust 'what next' thoughts/plans: Give details of planning for the future. What are you going to commit to?"*

A commitment is checkable. "I will manage my time better" is an aspiration. "I will audit data availability against the analysis plan before committing to a method" is a commitment.

## §4 — Learning CMR post-processing in MASS · 250 words

**Argument — reframe this from the old draft.** The difficulty was not that the interface was unfamiliar. It was that **you had no reference standard against which to judge your own output.** You could produce a segmentation and be unable to tell whether it was right.

That distinction is the whole value of this section. An unfamiliar interface is a complaint. An absent reference standard is a methodological problem you then solved.

**Facts available**
- No prior experience of medical image post-processing.
- Raw 4D-flow output is a large multi-dimensional dataset requiring manual correction before values can be extracted.
- Valve-plane misplacement propagates and invalidates the downstream result.
- You could complete a segmentation and not know whether the resulting values were physiologically plausible.
- Dr Garg and the team taught the workflow step by step, including the anatomical reason for each step rather than the click sequence alone.
- You used your Part A reading as a plausibility check on your own numbers.
- Your MASS workflow produced EDV, ESV, SV and LVEF alongside the flow-energetic parameters — these are checkable against expected physiological ranges.

**Questions**
1. What was the first thing you produced in MASS that you could not evaluate, and why couldn't you evaluate it?
2. How did you eventually establish whether your own output was trustworthy? Name the actual check.
3. What did being taught the anatomical reason for each step change about your ability to work independently on difficult scans?
4. **Answer for yourself before writing:** did you perform any repeat measurement on the same dataset to test your own consistency? If yes, that belongs here as evidence. If no, it belongs in the commitment instead.

**Cut / avoid**
- "I felt very frustrated and incompetent." Delete. It changes nothing a reader concludes about you.
- The Gantt chart. It moves to §5, where it does real work.

**Commitment:** for any future analysis software, a defined number of supervised repeat measurements plus a documented intra-observer consistency check before generating analysable data.

## §5 — Change of project scope: PV loops to 4D-flow · 350 words · CENTREPIECE

Slide 5 item 1 asks for "the key elements that impacted on you and your project, **especially if it was something you felt that was out of your control**." This is that. It is entirely absent from the March essay. It is your strongest material and it gets the largest budget.

**The structural point that matters most in this whole brief:** §4 and §5 are causally linked. The MASS learning curve consumed the schedule; the consumed schedule made the PV-loop plan unachievable. State that chain explicitly and two descriptive sections become one completed reflective cycle. Leave it implicit and you reproduce the exact failing in cohort feedback item 3.

This is why the Gantt chart belongs here, not in §4. It is the hinge: a plan on paper that did not account for the time cost of learning complex software from scratch.

**Argument, in three movements**
1. What the original plan was and why it was scientifically reasonable — non-invasive PV-loop reconstruction from CMR to assess myocardial energetics (stroke work, pressure-volume area, mechanical efficiency).
2. What made it unachievable — the fixed dissertation timeframe, against a schedule already absorbed by learning post-processing.
3. What you did — the decision process, jointly with your supervisor, and what it cost and preserved.

**Facts available**
- The original outcomes were stroke work, pressure-volume area and mechanical efficiency.
- The re-scoped study measures kinetic energy, vorticity and energy loss across FullRR, systolic, diastolic, E-wave and A-wave intervals.
- In the original plan, 4D-flow was a secondary sub-analysis. It became the entire study.
- Your reading on viscous energy loss and turbulent kinetic energy transferred into the new scope. PV-loop-specific reading did not.
- The re-scoped study completed, with a defensible finding: higher LV vorticity in AS during FullRR and systole.

**Questions**
1. What was the original question, and why was it worth asking?
2. When did you first have evidence the timeline would not hold, and how long was it before you acted on that evidence? (Answer this honestly — it is the most reflective question in the report.)
3. What were the decision criteria for re-scoping? Who decided what?
4. What work was lost, and what carried over?
5. **The judgement that earns the marks:** is the re-scoped question weaker, equivalent, or better supported by the data you actually held? Take a position and justify it. Refusing to answer is the only wrong option.

**Cut / avoid**
- Any attribution of fault. Frame as a joint decision under a real constraint.
- Presenting the pivot as a rescue or a happy accident. It cost you something; say what.

**Commitment:** audit data availability and method feasibility against the analysis plan *before* committing to a method — i.e. confirm the required inputs exist in the dataset at the planning stage, not the analysis stage.

## §6 — Analysis, interpretation and working with imperfect data · 230 words

**Argument:** the real intellectual work was deciding what could and could not be claimed from five versus five retrospective registry datasets — and choosing to state the constraints rather than smooth them.

This is the strongest evidence of research maturity available anywhere in your dissertation. Do not undersell it.

**Facts available — all from locked Part B, all defensible**
- Five participants per group. Statistical power severely limited.
- The historical selection mechanism could not be independently established, and was therefore **not** described as random, consecutive or formally matched.
- AS severity was not used as an eligibility criterion, a selection criterion, or a stratification variable.
- Controls were not prospectively recruited for this study and are clinically heterogeneous: two with AF, two with oedema, two NYHA class II, one current smoker, one ex-smoker.
- Absence of AS in the control group could not be independently re-established — no valve measurements were available for control datasets.
- Fifteen between-group comparisons, no multiplicity correction, nominal p < 0.05.
- FullRR and systolic vorticity are related measurements from the same velocity field, not two independent confirmations.
- You declined to import the research proposal's inclusion/exclusion criteria once you established they did not match the data actually held.

**Questions**
1. What was the first claim you wanted to make and then decided you could not support?
2. Why did you choose to state the selection mechanism as unestablished rather than describe it as consecutive or matched?
3. On the two significant vorticity results — what made you treat them as convergent rather than as independent confirmation? What does that show about how you read your own results?
4. Why did you reject the proposal's eligibility criteria, and what does that decision demonstrate?

**Cut / avoid**
- **Do not restate Part B's limitations paragraph.** The subject here is your decision process, not the limitations themselves. If a sentence would also fit in Discussion 4.5, it does not belong here.

**Commitment:** pre-specified analysis plans with a stated multiplicity strategy, fixed before outcome data are examined.

## Reflective model — one decision

The March essay name-drops Gibbs (2013) and "the experiential learning cycle" without completing a cycle. That is precisely what cohort feedback item 3 penalises.

- **Option A:** name a model and apply it consistently, so each section visibly moves description → evaluation → conclusion → commitment.
- **Option B:** name no model and rely on the structure.

Either is acceptable. The failure mode is naming a model decoratively. **Recommend A**, with the cycle carried by each section's shape rather than sub-labelled, and Gibbs cited once. Note that the eight sections above are already built to that shape — description, then evaluation, then a commitment.
