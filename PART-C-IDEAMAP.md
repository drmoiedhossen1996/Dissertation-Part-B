## 2. Cross-cutting threads

The spine gives narrative. Threads give **synthesis** — slide 5 item 6 asks for evaluation *and* synthesis, and synthesis is what happens when an idea recurs in a changed form across sections.

Pick two or three of these and let them recur deliberately. Do not try to run all four.

**T1 · Judgement under uncertainty**
Appears as: not knowing whether a segmentation was correct (§4) → not knowing whether a between-group difference was real (§6) → deciding what could be claimed (§6).
Synthesis payload: the same skill — establishing confidence in an unverifiable output — operating at two different scales.

**T2 · Time as a research resource**
Appears as: the proposal Gantt chart (§5) → time consumed by skill acquisition (§4) → the pivot decision (§5) → scheduling around capacity (§7).
Synthesis payload: time was the binding constraint on the science itself, not merely on your comfort.

**T3 · Verifying your own work**
Appears as: reference errors and the move to identifier import (§3) → physiological plausibility checks on MASS output (§4) → refusing to describe the selection mechanism as matched when it wasn't (§6).
Synthesis payload: research integrity as a set of process habits, not a disposition.

**T4 · From scaffolded to independent**
Appears as: supervisor direction on literature appraisal (§3) → team teaching the anatomical reason behind each step (§4) → the pivot as a joint decision (§5) → your own analytical decisions on what to claim (§6).
Synthesis payload: a traceable trajectory in how much of the judgement was yours.

**Recommended pair: T1 and T3.** They are the most evidenced, they are both about research quality rather than about you, and they support the "level of self-reflection" descriptor in the Senate scale most directly.

## 8. Reflective quality — what separates the bands

The Senate scale grades "level of self-reflection" explicitly. The distinguishing feature across bands is not honesty or detail. It is whether reflection produces a **changed method**.

| Level | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Weak | describes what happened and how it felt |
| Adequate | describes, then names a lesson |
| Strong | describes, evaluates why it happened, and identifies what it revealed about the process |
| Strongest | all of the above, and the process actually changed as a result — with the change stated specifically enough to be checked |

You have three genuine instances of a changed method to draw on: the reference-management change, the plausibility-checking of MASS output, and the decision to state the selection mechanism as unestablished. Use them as the anchors. They are worth more than any amount of narrative.
